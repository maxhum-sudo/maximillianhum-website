Where Onboarding Breaks — and What to Do About It

Product changes needed for Bet 01

I tried to bring Ramp to FascinAsian, a non-profit where I sit on the board. The product was the right fit — we needed better spend controls and a cleaner expense workflow. I started the self-service onboarding myself and hit three distinct walls. None of them were about product-market fit. All of them were solvable.

What follows is a breakdown of each friction point: exactly what happened, which customer segments share each failure mode, what the CS team can do right now, and what a product fix looks like.

The Three Friction Points
01
No handoff to the person with access
Bank connection · Accounting software integration
What happened

To complete onboarding I needed to connect bank accounts and accounting software. I'm not the treasurer — I don't have those credentials and I'm not the right person to connect them. There was no way to hand off that step to the person who does. The entire onboarding sat with me, incomplete.

Who else stalls here

Any organization where the person starting onboarding isn't the person with financial access. A structural pattern, not an edge case.

Non-profits (board member ≠ treasurer) SMBs with a bookkeeper Ops lead starts, finance owns accounts
CS motion — now

Proactive outreach at the bank connection step: "Does someone else own your banking access? We can send them a link to complete just this step." Recovers accounts with intent that are stuck on an internal handoff that never happens.

Product fix

An "invite a teammate" flow mid-onboarding: a scoped link letting the treasurer complete only the connection steps, without full admin access or starting over. The initiator stays in the loop; the right person completes their piece.

02
Signer date of birth — no context, no async path
KYC verification · Signer information
What happened

I was asked for the date of birth of the signing officer. I didn't have it. There was no explanation of why it was needed, and no option to collect it from the signer directly. The flow stopped.

Who else stalls here

Anyone onboarding on behalf of an organization where they aren't the designated signer — common in any structured entity.

Board members Operations leads Administrative staff Outsourced finance teams
CS motion — now

Pre-empt this at the start of the flow with a checklist of what's needed — including signer date of birth and the reason (KYC compliance). For accounts that stall here, follow up with a direct explanation and an async collection path.

Product fix

An in-flow sidebar explaining the KYC requirement with an async option: "Send your signing officer a link to verify their identity." Decouples the onboarding initiator from the verification requirement entirely.

03
Director upload doesn't scale past a handful
Ownership structure · Director verification
What happened

FascinAsian has 12 directors. The upload flow required individual submissions for each one. With no save-and-return option, starting meant committing to finishing. The time cost made completion feel impossible. I didn't finish.

Who else stalls here

Any entity with a larger board or ownership structure. Not rare — just higher-friction than the median SMB the flow was built for.

Non-profits with full boards Holding companies Partnerships Co-operatives Family offices
CS motion — now

When a customer indicates 5+ directors, trigger proactive outreach: "We noticed you have a larger ownership structure — here's the fastest way through this." A templated collection process turns a 30-minute friction point into a guided 10-minute task.

Product fix

Bulk upload or CSV import for director and owner information. A progress-save feature so multi-director onboarding can span sessions without losing earlier work. Session persistence alone would recover a meaningful share of these stalls.

The Common Thread
The flow was built for a single decision-maker with immediate access to everything.

Most micro-SMBs fit that profile. But a meaningful segment doesn't — and right now, all three customer types fall off at the same cliff: the moment onboarding requires information or access that belongs to someone else in the organization. The fixes aren't expensive. A pre-onboarding checklist, a teammate handoff link, and bulk director upload would recover a significant portion of accounts currently stalling in the first two weeks. None of these require rebuilding the flow — they're additive layers on what already exists.

Three Bets for the First 90 Days
01
Ship a "what you'll need" pre-onboarding checklist

Send every new signup a checklist of required information before they open the flow: bank credentials, signer date of birth, director list. Reduces surprise drop-off at all three friction points. Zero product work — this is an email and a help document.

Measure Drop-off rate at bank connection, signer verification, and director upload steps before and after.
02
Re-engage all accounts stalled at connection or verification

Identify every account that started onboarding and stalled at bank connection or signer verification. These are warm accounts with real intent — they just needed a bridge. Reach out directly with a handoff option and a clear next step. This is recoverable revenue sitting in the queue.

Measure Activation rate of re-engaged stalled accounts versus self-service baseline.
03
Route high-director-count signups to a dedicated activation path

Flag any account with 5+ directors at signup and route them to a higher-touch track: proactive outreach, a templated information collection process, and a dedicated call if needed. Prevents the friction that kills these accounts before they ever reach activation.

Measure 60-day activation rate for high-director accounts on the dedicated path versus self-service control group.
